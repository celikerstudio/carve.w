## What is TDEE?
Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) is the sum of BMR, physical activity, and thermic effect of food. It represents how many calories you burn daily.

:::takeaway
Estimate TDEE, then adjust intake by **100-200 kcal** based on **2-3 week** weight trends and performance.
:::

## How does it work?
TDEE = BMR + exercise + NEAT + TEF. NEAT can swing hundreds of calories daily depending on steps and fidgeting. Diets reduce TDEE through weight loss and lower NEAT; gaining weight increases it modestly. Matching intake to TDEE controls weight change pace.

## Practical application
Start with an estimate from calculators and activity multipliers. Track intake, weight averages, and steps for 2-3 weeks; if weight is stable, you’ve found maintenance. To cut, reduce 300-600 kcal; to gain, add 150-300 kcal. Keep steps consistent to reduce NEAT noise. Reassess after body weight shifts ~5%.

:::checklist
- Measure | Log calories, weight, and steps for **2-3 weeks** to find maintenance.
- Adjust | Change intake by **100-200 kcal** increments based on trends.
- Consistency | Hold activity steady to read TDEE clearly.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Trusting multipliers blindly without tracking outcomes.
- Ignoring NEAT changes that skew TDEE during diets or bulks.
- Making huge calorie jumps, overshooting goals.
