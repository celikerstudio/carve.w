## What is caffeine?
Caffeine is a stimulant that blocks adenosine receptors, increasing alertness and reducing perceived effort. It is one of the most researched ergogenic aids and can enhance strength, power, and endurance when dosed correctly.

:::takeaway
Dose **3-6 mg/kg** 30-60 minutes pre-workout; cap daily intake near **400 mg** and go lower for late sessions.
:::

## How does it work?
By blocking adenosine, caffeine boosts central nervous system activity and motor unit recruitment. It can increase pain tolerance and lower RPE, allowing higher [[intensity]] or [[volume]] in a session. It may spare some glycogen during longer efforts by increasing fatty acid availability. Effects peak within an hour and can disrupt [[sleep]] if taken late.
Individual responses vary based on adenosine receptor and CYP1A2 genetics, so testing dose and timing is essential.

## Practical application
Start with 3 mg/kg to assess tolerance, and only scale toward 6 mg/kg if benefits are clear. For evening training, use 1-3 mg/kg or skip to protect sleep. Track total intake from coffee, pre-workout powders, and energy drinks to stay within safe limits. Cycle lower doses on easier days to reduce habituation. If jitters occur, try smaller split doses or a lower-caffeine pre-workout paired with carbs for stable energy.
Pair caffeine with adequate water and electrolytes, especially in hot environments, to avoid compounding dehydration-driven fatigue.

:::checklist
- Dosing | Begin near **3 mg/kg**; only move toward **6 mg/kg** if needed and tolerated.
- Timing | Take it **30-60 min** pre-lift; reduce or skip for late sessions to safeguard sleep.
- Safety | Keep total intake under **400 mg/day** for most adults and monitor side effects.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Taking large doses late in the day, impairing sleep and recovery.
- Redosing mid-session without need, causing jitters or a crash.
- Using caffeine to mask poor programming or under-fueling instead of fixing [[calories]] and [[carbohydrates]].
