## What is bulking?
Bulking is a planned calorie surplus paired with resistance training to gain muscle mass. The goal is to add size with minimal fat by controlling surplus size and keeping training progressive.

:::takeaway
Use a **150-300 kcal/day** surplus to gain **0.25-0.5% body weight/week** while holding **1.6-2.2 g/kg** [[protein]] and steady [[progressive-overload]].
:::

## How does it work?
Extra calories provide energy and substrate for muscle protein synthesis. Paired with hard training, the surplus supports higher [[volume]] and [[intensity]] and improves recovery. Oversized surpluses mostly add fat, so rate control matters. Consistent protein and carbs bias the surplus toward lean gain.

## Practical application
Set protein 1.6-2.2 g/kg, fats 0.6-1.0 g/kg, fill the rest with carbs to fuel training. Track weight weekly; adjust surplus by 100-150 kcal if gain is too fast/slow. Keep steps and [[sleep]] consistent to interpret trends. Favor whole foods but use calorie-dense options (rice, oats, oils, nut butters) to hit targets without GI overload. Deload every 6-10 weeks to keep sensitivity and manage fatigue.

:::checklist
- Surplus size | Start **+150-300 kcal/day**; target **0.25-0.5%/week** gain.
- Macros | **1.6-2.2 g/kg** protein, **0.6-1.0 g/kg** fat, rest carbs for training fuel.
- Monitoring | Track weight, performance, and digestion; tweak calories in **100-150 kcal** steps.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Running huge surpluses that add mostly fat.
- Letting steps and sleep drift, muddying progress signals.
- Neglecting digestion—overeating low-density foods and missing targets.
