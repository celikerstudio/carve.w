## What is frequency?
Frequency is how often you train a muscle group or movement pattern each week. Spreading work across more sessions can improve skill practice, manage fatigue, and raise total quality volume while keeping recovery intact.

:::takeaway
Most lifters progress best with **2-3** sessions per muscle per week, sharing **10-20** hard sets to boost quality and recovery.
:::

## How does it work?
Higher frequency shortens recovery windows between stimuli, allowing you to accumulate effective [[volume]] with less fatigue per session. More practice on key lifts improves motor learning and execution, supporting [[progressive-overload]]. It also smooths soreness and joint stress by avoiding massive single-day workloads that can hurt [[sleep]] and adherence.
Beginners benefit from extra exposure for faster skill acquisition, while advanced lifters often need frequency to fit higher weekly volumes without crushing any single day.

## Practical application
Distribute weekly sets (10-20 per muscle) across at least two days; e.g., push/pull/legs or upper/lower splits. Place heavy, technical work when freshest and accessories later. Rotate session emphases (e.g., strength focus day, hypertrophy focus day) to manage fatigue. If recovery lags, reduce per-session sets before cutting weekly frequency. Keep 1-3 reps in reserve on most sets to sustain quality across sessions.
If stuck at two days, micro-dose extras: add 1-2 easy sets of a lagging muscle after another session to raise quality volume without a full extra day.

:::checklist
- Set allocation | Split **10-20 sets** per muscle into **2-3** sessions to keep quality high.
- Session focus | Lead with compounds when fresh; accessories later for safer fatigue.
- Recovery check | If sore or flat, trim per-session sets before lowering frequency entirely.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Cramming all volume into one day, spiking fatigue and soreness.
- Jumping to 4-5 weekly touches without first improving sleep, nutrition, and load management.
- Letting technique drift across the week by pushing every session to failure.
