## What are testosterone boosters?
“Test boosters” are supplements claiming to raise testosterone for muscle/strength gains. Most legal products have minimal impact in healthy men.

:::takeaway
For healthy adults, boosters rarely move testosterone meaningfully; prioritize sleep, nutrition, training, and medical care if deficient.
:::

## How does it work?
Some ingredients (e.g., D-aspartic acid, fenugreek, tribulus) show little consistent effect. Zinc/vitamin D help only if deficient. Real fixes for low testosterone involve sleep, stress control, adequate [[calories]], and clinical evaluation.

## Practical application
If symptoms of low T, get labs and address sleep (7-9h), body weight, and nutrition first. Correct deficiencies (vitamin D, zinc) under guidance. Avoid expensive blends with bold claims. Train with [[progressive-overload]], manage stress, and consider medical consult if labs stay low.

:::checklist
- Assess | Test clinically if symptoms persist; avoid self-diagnosis.
- Foundations | Sleep, calories, resistance training, micronutrients.
- Skepticism | Most boosters have trivial effects; spend on diet/training instead.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Relying on boosters instead of fixing sleep, diet, and training.
- Using untested blends with unknown safety.
- Skipping medical evaluation when symptoms are present.
