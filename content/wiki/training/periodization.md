## What is periodization?
Periodization is planning training in phases to manage fatigue and progression over time. It organizes variables like [[volume]], [[intensity]], and frequency so you keep adapting instead of stalling or overreaching.

:::takeaway
Use blocks of **4-8 weeks** with a clear focus (e.g., hypertrophy vs strength), then deload and shift emphasis while keeping core lifts consistent.
:::

## How does it work?
Shifting stressors across blocks (more volume, then more intensity) prevents plateaus by resensitizing muscles and the nervous system. Planned deloads manage fatigue so performance rebounds. Stable exercise selection preserves skill while variable rep ranges and loading schemes keep stimulus novel but controlled.

## Practical application
Pick 1-2 priorities per block (e.g., higher reps and sets for size, then lower reps for strength). Keep 2-3 main lifts consistent, rotate close variations as needed for joints. Progress load/reps weekly within the block, then deload 30-50% volume before the next phase. Track RPE to avoid overshooting. Align nutrition: slight surplus for high-volume blocks, maintenance or mild deficit for lighter phases if cutting.

:::checklist
- Block focus | Define **hypertrophy** vs **strength** emphasis per **4-8 week** block.
- Progression | Increase load/reps weekly, then deload before the next phase.
- Consistency | Keep core lifts stable; swap close variants only to manage joints/fatigue.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Changing all exercises weekly, losing skill and progression.
- Never deloading, letting fatigue mask performance and adaptation.
- Mixing too many goals at once, blurring progression and recovery.
