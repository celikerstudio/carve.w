## What is massage?
Massage applies pressure and manipulation to soft tissues to reduce tension, promote relaxation, and aid perceived recovery.

:::takeaway
Use massage to reduce tension and stress; it complements—does not replace—sleep, nutrition, and smart programming.
:::

## How does it work?
Massage can improve blood flow, reduce muscle tone, and lower stress perception. Benefits are often subjective but can help relaxation and readiness when combined with good recovery habits. It does not “flush lactic acid” but may ease discomfort.

## Practical application
Schedule massage after hard blocks or when tension accumulates. Communicate pressure preferences. Hydrate before/after. Use self-massage tools between sessions. If sore spots persist, adjust training load and technique rather than relying solely on massage.

:::checklist
- Timing | After hard blocks or when tension limits movement.
- Pressure | Communicate needs; avoid excessive pain.
- Integration | Pair with load management, [[sleep]], and nutrition.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Using massage to ignore program or recovery issues.
- Accepting painful pressure that worsens soreness.
- Skipping hydration or movement afterward.
