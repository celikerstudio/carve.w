## What are fats?
Dietary fats provide essential fatty acids, support hormone production, and aid absorption of fat-soluble vitamins. They contribute to satiety and overall health. For lifters, adequate fat intake helps maintain hormonal balance that supports muscle gain and recovery.

:::takeaway
Keep fats near **0.6-1.0 g/kg** daily; favor unsaturated sources and steady omega-3 intake.
:::

## How does it work?
Fats slow digestion and steady energy, improving appetite control. Omega-3s reduce inflammation and may aid recovery from training stress. Adequate fat supports testosterone and other hormones involved in adaptation. Fats complement carbs and [[protein]] within total [[calories]] to meet performance and body composition goals.
Dietary fat also carries fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, and K, which support immunity, bone health, and energy production for training.

## Practical application
Set fats lower (around 0.6 g/kg) when carbs are high to fuel frequent hard sessions; move toward 1.0 g/kg if you prefer lower-carb days or need more satiety in a deficit. Prioritize olive oil, nuts, seeds, avocado, eggs, and fatty fish for omega-3s. Keep trans fats near zero and saturated fat moderate. Distribute fats across meals to manage hunger, but keep pre-workout meals lighter in fat to avoid sluggishness. Consider an omega-3 supplement if fatty fish is rare in your diet.

:::checklist
- Daily range | Use **0.6-1.0 g/kg** and adjust with carb needs and appetite.
- Sources | Favor olive oil, nuts, seeds, avocado, eggs, and fatty fish for omega-3s.
- Timing | Keep pre-workout meals lower in fat to stay light during training.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Cutting fats too low during a deficit, which can impair hormones and adherence.
- Pushing fats very high while carbs stay high, overshooting calories and slowing recomposition.
- Ignoring omega-3 intake; aim for fatty fish twice weekly or supplement.
