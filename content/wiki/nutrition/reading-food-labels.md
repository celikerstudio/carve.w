## What is reading food labels?
Reading food labels is interpreting nutrition panels to track calories, macros, and ingredients. It helps align intake with goals and avoid hidden calories.

:::takeaway
Check **serving size**, **calories**, **protein/carbs/fat**, and note **fiber** and added sugars to align with targets.
:::

## How does it work?
Labels list per-serving nutrition and ingredients. Understanding them prevents underestimating calories or missing macro goals. Fiber and protein content affect satiety. Ingredient order shows prominence; long ultra-processed lists may mean lower satiety per calorie.

## Practical application
Confirm serving size and how many you actually eat. Track protein to hit **1.6-2.2 g/kg**, watch fats and carbs to fit your macro plan. Note fiber for digestion. Compare similar products for protein-per-calorie. For oils, sauces, and snacks, measure portions—they’re calorie dense. Use labels plus a food scale for accuracy.

:::checklist
- Serving reality | Compare label serving to your portion; measure dense foods.
- Macros | Ensure protein and fiber fit goals; watch fats/carbs for calories.
- Ingredients | Prefer simpler lists and lower added sugars when possible.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Ignoring serving sizes and undercounting calorie-dense foods.
- Tracking calories but missing low protein and fiber, hurting satiety.
- Over-relying on “health” branding without checking numbers.
