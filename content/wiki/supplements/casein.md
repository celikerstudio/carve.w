## What is casein?

Casein is the main protein in milk; the other is [[whey-protein]]. When acid is added to milk, the casein clumps together and sinks, while the whey stays on top [1]. Casein does the same in your stomach: it clumps and is digested more slowly than whey [1]. That is why it is called a slow protein.

:::takeaway
Casein is a good protein, but not a special one. Taking **30 to 40 g before bed** raises muscle protein building during the night [1], a short-term measure, not muscle growth. In a trial where both groups ate the **same total protein**, taking it before bed instead of during the day made no difference to muscle growth [4]. Get your daily total first; see [[protein]].
:::

## Is casein better than whey?

Not for building muscle in the hours after a meal. In a study in young men, whey stimulated about twice as much muscle protein building as casein in the hours after the drink, at rest and after training [2]. Whey is also slightly richer in leucine, at about 11% against 9.3% for casein [1].

Casein's advantage is that it lasts longer. In early studies, amino acids from casein rose slowly and were still above normal after 5 hours, while whey peaked and was back to baseline by then [1]. That makes casein a logical choice for a long stretch without food, like the night.

## Does casein before bed help you build muscle?

The idea is sound, but the long-term evidence is weaker than the marketing.

Over a single night it works: 30 to 40 g of casein before sleep raises the rate of muscle protein building, measured over the hours of that night, in young men and in older men (who got it through a stomach tube) [1]. That is a short-term measure. It does not show that you end up with more muscle. The ISSN, a sports nutrition society, also states that it raises the next morning's metabolic rate without blocking the breakdown of fat [1]. In that study, whey and carbohydrate before bed raised the morning metabolic rate too, so it is not something only casein does [1].

Over months, the picture is less clear. The best-known trial gave 44 young men 27.5 g of protein every night before sleep during 12 weeks of training. They gained more strength and more muscle than a group that got a drink without calories [3]. But the protein group also ate more protein in total: about 1.9 g per kg a day against 1.3 g per kg [1]. So the extra gains may simply come from more protein, not from the timing.

Two later trials point the same way:

- In 13 men who all ate 1.8 g of protein per kg a day, 35 g of casein during the day or before bed gave the same gains in muscle size and strength over 10 weeks [4]. Same total protein, different timing, same result. It was a small, preliminary study.
- In 30 older men who already ate at least 1.0 g per kg a day, an extra 40 g of protein before sleep or after training added nothing to the gains from 12 weeks of training alone [6].

A systematic review of nine studies reached the same careful conclusion: casein before sleep raises overnight protein building, may help muscle growth in young men, but the studies often compared groups with unequal protein intakes [5].

## Does casein before bed make you fat?

There is no sign of that. The ISSN reports that casein before sleep did not reduce fat breakdown overnight [1]. A systematic review of 11 studies found little to no effect of 24 to 48 g of casein before sleep on energy use, fat breakdown, appetite or how much people ate the next day [7].

That also means the claim works both ways. The ISSN's line about a higher morning metabolic rate comes from small studies [1], and the review found the effects on metabolism limited [7]. Casein before bed is not a fat-loss tool. It is 30 to 40 g of protein, about 120 to 160 kcal, that counts toward your day like any other food.

## Should you take casein?

Only if it helps you reach your daily protein. If you eat enough during the day, a casein shake at night adds little [4][6]. If you find it hard to eat enough, or you are hungry in the evening, a casein shake before bed is an easy way to fill the gap.

:::checklist
- Daily total first | Aim for your total protein for the day; see [[protein]].
- Before bed | **30 to 40 g** of casein is the amount studied [1].
- Count it | It has calories like any other protein; count it in your day.
- Dairy | Casein comes from milk. Skip it if you have a milk allergy.
:::

## Common mistakes

- **Adding it on top of an intake that is already enough.** In men who all ate 1.8 g per kg a day, timing made no difference [4], and in older untrained men eating at least 1.0 g per kg, an extra 40 g added nothing [6].
- **Expecting it to burn fat at night.** The studies show no fat-loss effect worth counting on [7].
- **Buying it because of the timing.** With the same total protein, a small trial found no difference between day and night [4].
