## What is a 1RM?
One Rep Max (1RM) is the maximum load you can lift once with good form. It guides strength programming and percentages but should be tested wisely to manage risk and fatigue.

:::takeaway
Test **sparingly**; use estimated 1RM from recent top sets at known RPE to guide training instead of frequent max attempts.
:::

## How does it work?
A true 1RM requires maximal motor unit recruitment and precise technique. Testing is fatiguing and can disrupt training if overused. Estimated 1RM from submax sets (e.g., RPE 8-9 singles or AMRAPs) tracks strength with less risk and time. Percent-based or RPE-based loading then scales from that estimate.

## Practical application
Test true 1RM after a taper or deload, with a thorough warm-up and clear spotters/safeties. More often, use an RPE 8-9 single or a rep-max set and plug reps and load into an e1RM calculator. Update estimates every 3-6 weeks. Program main work at percentages or RPE derived from e1RM and adjust loads day-to-day using RPE if readiness shifts.

:::checklist
- Frequency | Prefer **e1RM** from submax sets; save true max tests for key checkpoints.
- Setup | Warm up gradually; use spotters/safeties; stop if form degrades.
- Loading | Base percentages on e1RM; adjust with [[rpe]] to account for readiness.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Maxing weekly, accumulating fatigue and risking injury.
- Testing without safeties or spotters.
- Ignoring technique breakdown when chasing a number, making the result unreliable.
