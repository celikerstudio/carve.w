## What is warming up?
Warming up prepares your body and nervous system for training by increasing temperature, mobility, and muscle activation. A targeted warm-up improves performance and reduces injury risk.

:::takeaway
Use **5-10 minutes** of light cardio plus **ramp-up sets** on your first lifts; keep it specific, not exhausting.
:::

## How does it work?
Raising temperature and blood flow enhances force production and joint viscosity. Rehearsing movement patterns grooves technique and improves stability. Progressive ramp-up sets let you find the day’s working loads while monitoring [[rpe]] and readiness.
Warm joints and elastic tissues lower injury risk, while neural activation improves motor unit recruitment. Good warm-ups also give a quick readiness check—if ramp sets feel unusually heavy, adjust loads or volume before work sets.

## Practical application
Do 5-10 minutes of low-intensity cardio or dynamic moves, then specific mobility for tight areas. For each main lift, take 2-4 ramp-up sets from empty bar to working weight, increasing load while keeping reps low. Stop warm-ups if fatigue builds—save energy for work sets. If tight, insert brief pauses or tempo work in the first set only.
Prioritize patterns you will train: hinge-focused warm-ups before deadlifts, scapular prep before pressing. For time-crunched days, combine mobility with ramp-up sets (e.g., pauses in the hole) instead of adding extra drills.
When changing climates (cold gym, early mornings), add an extra light ramp set to feel ready before loading heavier weights. Reuse a consistent warm-up so you can tell when a day feels off.

:::checklist
- General prep | **5-10 min** light cardio or dynamic movement.
- Specific ramp | 2-4 ramp-up sets to working weight; keep reps low and crisp.
- Focus | Prioritize the first big lift; avoid turning warm-up into a workout.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Skipping ramp-up sets and jumping to heavy loads cold.
- Overdoing mobility or cardio, draining energy for main work.
- Using random drills unrelated to the day’s movements.
