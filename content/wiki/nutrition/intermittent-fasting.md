## What is intermittent fasting?

Intermittent fasting means you go without food for set periods. The ISSN, a sports nutrition society, describes three main forms [1]:

- Alternate-day fasting: a fasting day (no food, or very little) alternates with a normal day.
- Whole-day fasting: one or two fasting days a week, such as the 5:2 diet.
- Time-restricted eating: you fast for 16 to 20 hours and eat within a window of 4 to 8 hours every day, such as 16:8.

:::takeaway
Intermittent fasting works when it helps you eat less. On the whole, it gives **no clear advantage over cutting calories every day** for losing fat [1][2], and it may cost slightly more lean mass [3]. Choose it if it suits you, and keep your protein and training up [1].
:::

## Does intermittent fasting help you lose more fat?

Not more than a normal calorie deficit. The ISSN concludes that research on intermittent fasting as a whole shows no significant advantage over daily calorie restriction for body composition [1].

Newer studies say much the same. A 2025 network meta-analysis of 99 trials in 6,582 adults, most of whom (5,862) had an existing health condition, found that every form of fasting and normal calorie restriction led to weight loss compared with no diet. Only alternate-day fasting beat normal restriction, by 1.29 kg, and only in shorter trials. In trials lasting 24 weeks or more, no form of fasting did better than normal restriction [2]. Another meta-analysis, of 28 trials in healthy adults, found that the fasting forms combined did no better than normal restriction for weight or fat. Time-restricted eating on its own lost a bit more weight and fat, but also more fat-free mass [3].

Two longer trials show what that looks like in practice. In 139 people with obesity who all followed the same calorie target for a year, eating only between 8 am and 4 pm gave 8.0 kg of weight loss against 6.3 kg without a time window. That difference was not significant [4]. In 100 adults with obesity, alternate-day fasting gave the same weight loss over a year as eating 25% less every day, and more people quit the fasting diet: 38% against 29% [5].

## Why does it work for some people?

Because a shorter eating window can make it easier to eat less. In a review of trials the ISSN discusses, fasting was better at suppressing hunger than daily restriction [1]. In seven trials in people who lifted weights, those on a time-restricted schedule ate about 175 kcal a day less than those who ate normally [7].

But the window does not do the work on its own. In a 12-week trial in 116 adults with overweight or obesity, eating only from noon to 8 pm, without any other change, gave no more weight loss than three meals a day [6]. If fasting makes your deficit easier to keep, that is a good reason to use it. If you end up eating the same amount in less time, it will not help. See [[calorie-deficit]] and [[hunger-and-satiety]].

## Do you lose muscle with intermittent fasting?

Possibly a little more. The meta-analysis of 28 trials above found that fasting diets lost slightly more fat-free mass than normal restriction, about 0.20 kg [3]. In the noon-to-8-pm trial above, the 50 people who were measured in person showed that the fasting group lost more lean mass in their arms and legs [6].

With strength training and enough protein, the picture looks better. A meta-analysis of seven small trials in people who lifted found that time-restricted eating gave more fat loss and no difference in muscle or strength [7]. Two of those trials give an idea of the setup. In 34 trained men who ate the same calories, a 16:8 schedule for 8 weeks led to more fat loss while muscle and strength stayed the same, though their testosterone dropped [8]. In trained women eating 1.6 g of protein per kg a day, an eating window of about 7.5 hours gave the same gains in muscle and strength as eating normally [9].

The ISSN adds a caveat: it is not yet clear whether fasting could beat spreading your food over the day for maximum muscle and strength, and fasting needs caution and careful planning if you need peak athletic performance [1].

## How do you make it work if you lift?

Keep the basics in place. The ISSN names adequate protein, resistance training and an appropriate rate of weight loss as what protects your muscle during fat loss [1]. Its protein position stand advises spreading protein over the day, ideally every 3 to 4 hours [10]. In an 8-hour window that means two or three protein-rich meals. See [[protein]] for how much.

If lifting on an empty stomach feels bad, move your workout into your eating window or just before it. See [[pre-workout-nutrition]].

## Who should be careful with fasting?

Do not fast if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, have had an eating disorder, or are underweight (a BMI below 18.5) [11]. A review of fasting in clinical practice also advises against it for children under 12 and for teenagers of normal weight, and says people over 70 should be careful, because it is unclear what fasting does to the muscle you lose with age [11].

If you have type 1 or type 2 diabetes, fasting may be possible, but only with close monitoring by your doctor [11]. If you take medication that has to go with meals at set times, work out a fasting schedule with your doctor first [11].

## Your checklist

:::checklist
- Goal | Use fasting to make your calorie deficit easier, not as a fat-burning trick.
- Window | Pick a schedule you can keep around work, family and training.
- Protein | Reach your daily protein within the window, in two or three solid meals.
- Training | Keep lifting; train in or near your eating window if fasted sessions feel weak.
- Check | Eating the same amount in less time? Then fasting is not doing anything for you.
:::
