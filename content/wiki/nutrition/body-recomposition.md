## What is body recomposition?
Body recomposition is losing fat and gaining muscle at the same time. It relies on smart nutrition, high-quality training, and patience—progress is slower than a dedicated bulk or cut.

:::takeaway
Use a **small deficit or maintenance**, hit **1.6-2.4 g/kg** [[protein]], and train hard with [[progressive-overload]] to shift fat to muscle over months.
:::

## How does it work?
Slightly under or at maintenance calories with high protein and resistance training encourages fat use while supporting muscle repair. Beginners, detrained lifters, and higher-body-fat individuals see faster recomposition. Adequate sleep and consistent [[volume]] enable muscle retention and growth despite modest calories.

## Practical application
Eat at maintenance or a 5-10% deficit. Set protein 1.6-2.4 g/kg, fats 0.6-0.9 g/kg, fill the rest with carbs to fuel [[intensity]]. Track weekly weight, waist, and training loads. If strength rises and waist drops, stay the course; if stalled, adjust calories by 100-150. Prioritize sleep 7-9 hours and keep steps consistent to avoid hidden activity drops.

:::checklist
- Calories | Maintain or run a **5-10% deficit**; adjust slowly.
- Protein | **1.6-2.4 g/kg** with 3-5 feedings.
- Training | Lift 3-5x/week, push load/reps, deload when fatigue accumulates.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Using a deep deficit, losing muscle and stalling strength.
- Under-eating protein and carbs, flattening training quality.
- Changing plans weekly instead of tracking trends over 3-4 weeks.
