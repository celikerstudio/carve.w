## What is a warm-up?

A warm-up is the preparation you do before you train. In 2026 a panel of 23 experts from 12 countries published what they call the first international consensus on warm-up in sport. They defined it as a structured and purposeful preparation to get you ready for training or competition [1]. Keep in mind who the panel had in mind: athletes in sport, not people training in a gym.

:::takeaway
Experts in sport say that **how complete a warm-up is** matters for how well it works, and they found **no support for protection against injury** in the short term [1]. For healthy adults, the ACSM says a **dynamic warm-up** is better than stretching for lifting performance, and that a warm-up **may** lower injury risk, though controlled studies are lacking [4].
:::

## Is there a guideline for warming up before lifting?

The ACSM position stands on resistance training, the 2009 one and the 2026 one that replaced it, do not mention warming up [2][3]. The ACSM's 2011 position stand on exercise for healthy adults does. It says a warm-up, a cool-down, flexibility exercise and a gradual build-up of volume and intensity may reduce the risk of heart problems and injuries during exercise, and rates that evidence category C, below its A and B [4]. It adds that controlled studies showing that a warm-up works for injuries are insufficient [4]. The first international consensus on warm-up in sport only appeared in 2026 [1].

## What makes a warm-up work?

The panel described a warm-up with three layers: general exercises, specific exercises, and conditioning activities. The last are voluntary muscle contractions with enough volume and intensity to induce a short-term boost in performance (postactivation performance enhancement) [1]. Readiness and fatigue can be checked during the warm-up with different tools [1].

The panel agreed that completeness is a key factor in how well a warm-up works [1]. The panel also stressed that a warm-up depends on the context and the sport [1], so there is no single routine to copy.

## Does a warm-up protect you from injury?

The expert panel found no support for protection against injury in the short term. It did say that some types of exercise in the warm-up may reduce injury risk over the long term, as they may in other parts of a training session [1]. The ACSM says a warm-up may lower the risk of injury during exercise, and that controlled studies are lacking [4]. See [[injury-prevention]] for what is known more broadly.

## Should you stretch before lifting?

For most people, the ACSM says a dynamic warm-up is better than flexibility exercise for improving cardiorespiratory or resistance exercise performance, especially when the exercise has many repetitions or lasts long [4].

A separate panel of 20 stretching experts, writing about healthy people, does not recommend long static stretching (more than 60 seconds per muscle) before maximal or explosive efforts in a single muscle group. Static calf stretching before maximal strength efforts is their example. They say that short static stretches inside a dynamic warm-up, and dynamic stretching, do not have that drawback [5]. More on stretching in [[stretching]].

## What can you do in the gym?

A common way to apply this: a few minutes of easy movement, then lighter sets of your first exercise, building up to your working weight, so your first heavy set is not your first set. If those lighter sets feel unusually heavy, that tells you something about the day. See [[rpe]].

:::checklist
- Definition | A structured, purposeful preparation for training [1].
- Completeness | Experts link a more complete warm-up to better results [1].
- Injury | No support for short-term protection [1]; a warm-up may lower injury risk, studies are lacking [4].
- Stretching | A dynamic warm-up beats stretching for lifting performance [4]; avoid long static stretches (over 60 s per muscle) before maximal efforts [5].
- In the gym | Easy movement, then lighter sets of your first exercise.
:::

## Common mistakes

- **Treating a warm-up as a guarantee.** The panel found no support for protection against injury in the short term, and the ACSM says controlled studies are lacking [1][4].
- **Holding long static stretches right before a maximal or explosive effort.** Panel advice: not over 60 seconds per muscle before maximal efforts [5].
- **Turning the warm-up into a workout.** Keep the lighter sets light, so you have your effort for the work sets.
