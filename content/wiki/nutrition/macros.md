## What are macronutrients?
Macronutrients are protein, carbohydrates, and fats—the calorie-providing nutrients that fuel performance and recovery. Balancing macros lets you hit total [[calories]] while supporting muscle gain, training quality, and adherence.

:::takeaway
Common starting points: **1.6-2.2 g/kg protein**, **3-5 g/kg carbs** (goal-dependent), **0.6-1.0 g/kg fats**, adjusted to hit calories.
:::

## How does it work?
Protein supplies amino acids for repair and [[hypertrophy]]. Carbs replenish glycogen to power [[volume]] and [[intensity]]. Fats support hormones and absorb fat-soluble vitamins. Total calories drive weight change; macros tune the quality of that change (muscle retention/gain vs. fat gain/loss). Consistent macro targets also stabilize energy and mood, reinforcing [[consistency]].

## Practical application
Set calories to match your goal (surplus, maintenance, or deficit). Set protein first (1.6-2.2 g/kg). Choose fats next (0.6-1.0 g/kg) based on satiety and carb preference. Fill the remainder with carbs to fuel training. Distribute protein across 3-5 meals with ~20-40 g each. Bias carbs around training (20-60 g pre/post), and keep some fats away from pre-lift meals to aid digestion. Adjust macros using weekly weight trends and gym performance.
Watch fiber (10-15 g per 1,000 kcal) to keep digestion steady without bloating training meals. Align macro timing with life: higher-carb on long or hard days, slightly lower on rest days while keeping protein stable.

:::checklist
- Protein floor | Hit **1.6-2.2 g/kg** daily, split over **3-5** meals.
- Fat range | Keep fats around **0.6-1.0 g/kg** for hormones and satiety.
- Carb fuel | Use remaining calories for carbs; place **20-60 g** pre/post to support training.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Setting protein too low, losing muscle in a deficit.
- Running very low carbs while demanding high training outputs.
- Ignoring weekly data; not adjusting macros when weight and performance trend the wrong way.
