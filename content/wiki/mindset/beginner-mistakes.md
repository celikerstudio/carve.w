## What are common beginner mistakes?
Beginners often struggle with exercise selection, execution, and recovery habits. These errors slow progress and increase frustration. Fixing them early accelerates results and reduces injury risk.

:::takeaway
Prioritize **6-8 core lifts**, **2-4 sets** each at **1-3 RIR**, and hit **1.6-2.2 g/kg** [[protein]] with **7-9 hours** of [[sleep]].
:::

## How does it work?
Poor technique limits tension on target muscles and increases joint stress. Random workouts without planned [[volume]] or [[intensity]] make [[progressive-overload]] impossible to track. Skipping [[protein]] and under-fueling [[calories]] impair recovery, while inadequate [[sleep]] blunts adaptation. Addressing these basics builds a foundation for long-term progress and confidence under the bar.

## Practical application
Learn movement patterns with lighter loads and film key lifts to review form. Follow a simple program with 6-8 core lifts, 2-4 sets each, 2-3 times per week, adding small progressions weekly. Keep most sets 1-3 reps in reserve to practice quality while progressing load or reps. Aim for 1.6-2.2 g/kg protein, consistent mealtimes, and 7-9 hours of sleep. Warm up with 5-10 minutes of light cardio plus specific ramp-up sets for each lift. Track sessions to spot trends and celebrate small wins.
Focus on bracing, neutral spine, and full range of motion before chasing heavier loads; quality reps compound faster than sporadic PR attempts.

:::checklist
- Program basics | Use **6-8** staple lifts, **2-4 sets** each, **2-3x/week** with 1-3 RIR.
- Nutrition | Hit **1.6-2.2 g/kg** protein and consistent meals to support recovery.
- Preparation | Warm up 5-10 minutes, film key sets, and log sessions to guide progression.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Maxing out weekly instead of accumulating quality sets near failure.
- Switching programs every few weeks, preventing skill development and measurement.
- Neglecting warm-ups, bracing, and range of motion, raising injury risk and limiting progress.
