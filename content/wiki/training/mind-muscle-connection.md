## What is mind-muscle connection?
Mind-muscle connection (MMC) is the deliberate focus on contracting the target muscle during an exercise. It improves technique and fiber recruitment, especially for hypertrophy and isolation work.

:::takeaway
Use controlled reps, stable setups, and **1-3 RIR** to feel the target muscle; film key sets to verify tension stays where intended.
:::

## How does it work?
Focusing on the target muscle can increase activation in EMG studies, particularly in isolation lifts and moderate loads. Better joint alignment and bracing reduce energy leaks so tension stays on the goal muscle. MMC is a complement to load and [[progressive-overload]], not a replacement.
Stronger MMC can improve force in the target muscle with the same load, making each rep more effective. Over time, it helps you identify when supporting muscles or momentum hijack a movement, so you can adjust cues or exercise choice.

## Practical application
Stabilize your body (benches, pads, bracing) before each set. Use moderate loads and controlled tempo (2-3s eccentrics) to feel the target. Limit momentum; pause briefly at peak contraction on accessories. Start sessions with a primer set for lagging muscles to groove the pattern. Review video to confirm joint angles and bar path.
Add simple cues like “drive elbows toward hips” on rows or “press through mid-foot” on squats to steer tension. If feel disappears mid-set, slow the eccentric for a rep or two to recenter technique, then resume normal tempo.

:::checklist
- Setup | Lock in pads, benches, and bracing to reduce movement.
- Tempo & load | Moderate loads with **2-3s** eccentrics to keep tension on target.
- Feedback | Film and adjust until the target muscle works hardest through the range.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Chasing feel with very light loads, never approaching failure.
- Letting momentum or loose setups steal tension from the target muscle.
- Ignoring overload—MMC should ride alongside measurable load or rep progress.
