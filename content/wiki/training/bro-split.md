## What is a bro split?
A bro split trains one major muscle group per day (e.g., chest day, back day), usually 5 days per week. It concentrates session volume on a single area while lowering per-muscle frequency.

:::takeaway
Use bro splits with **high quality sets** and keep **10 to 20 sets per muscle per week**. Training a muscle once a week is fine: at the same weekly sets, how often you train it made no difference to muscle growth. See [[frequency]].
:::

## How does it work?
Heavy same-day volume can drive [[hypertrophy]] if sets are near failure. Training a muscle once a week is not a problem in itself: with the same weekly sets, one day or more than five days a week made no difference to muscle growth (see [[frequency]]). The catch is the total. If one day cannot fit enough hard sets for a muscle, add a few on another day. Bro splits can be joint-friendly when fatigue per session is managed.

## Practical application
Run 4-6 exercises for the target muscle, 3-4 sets each, 6-15 reps, keeping 1-3 RIR. If a muscle falls short of its weekly sets, add a few sets for it on another day. Prioritize compounds early, then isolations. Monitor weekly set totals to stay near 10-20 per muscle. When soreness lingers and performance stalls, take a lighter week. A fixed deload schedule is a habit, not a finding; see [[deload]].

:::checklist
- Volume | Keep **10-20 sets/muscle/week**, even if split across one or two days.
- Once a week | Fine for growth at the same weekly sets. Add a second day only when one day cannot fit the total.
- Effort | Most sets **1-3 RIR**; avoid turning every session into all-out failure.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Hammering one day to exhaustion with poor-quality later sets.
- Giving a muscle too few sets because it only gets one day. Count the weekly total and add sets elsewhere if it falls short.
- Ignoring recovery and sleep needs after very high single-day volumes.
