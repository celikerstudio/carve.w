## What is discipline vs motivation?
Motivation is the fluctuating desire to act; discipline is the system of habits and commitments that keep you acting when motivation dips. Training success depends more on disciplined structures than on feeling motivated.

:::takeaway
Lock a **3-5 day** schedule, pre-commit **time/place/plan**, and use cues and checklists so action happens even when motivation is low.
:::

## How does it work?
Discipline relies on habits, environment design, and pre-commitments that reduce friction. Implementation intentions (if-then plans) link triggers to behaviors, making sessions more automatic. Motivation spikes are useful to start, but disciplined routines ensure [[consistency]] across weeks, sustaining [[progressive-overload]] and nutrition habits like steady [[protein]] intake.
Reducing choice overload—fewer program changes, standard shopping lists, and a fixed gym time—keeps willpower demands low so you can repeat actions even under stress.

## Practical application
Set fixed training days and times; prepare clothes, shoes, and a simple session plan the day prior. Use small starter actions (5-minute warm-up) to overcome inertia. Track 1-2 key metrics weekly (loads, steps, protein) to maintain feedback. Reduce decision fatigue with default meals and a short list of go-to workouts for busy days. Pair accountability with reminders, partners, or coaching check-ins.
If motivation drops, shrink the target to a “minimum viable session” (e.g., two compounds, two sets each) to preserve habit continuity and data for next adjustments.

:::checklist
- Pre-commit | Fix **days/times** and prep gear and a brief plan the night before.
- Starter action | Begin with a **5-minute** warm-up to break inertia and continue.
- Defaults | Keep default meals and a backup short session for hectic days to protect adherence.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Relying on motivation spikes instead of building repeatable routines.
- Overcomplicating plans, raising friction and missed sessions.
- Dropping all training on busy days instead of using shorter fallback sessions.
