## What is flexible dieting?
Flexible dieting (“If It Fits Your Macros”) emphasizes hitting calorie and macro targets with food choice flexibility. It prioritizes outcomes while allowing personal preferences to improve adherence.

:::takeaway
Meet daily calories and macros (esp. [[protein]]), emphasize whole foods **80-90%**, and use flexibility for the remaining **10-20%**.
:::

## How does it work?
Body composition responds mainly to calories, macros, and training. Allowing some discretionary foods can reduce cravings and improve long-term consistency. Micronutrient and fiber needs still matter; flexibility is a buffer, not a replacement for diet quality.

## Practical application
Set calories/macros for your goal. Build most meals from whole foods: lean proteins, starches/fruit, veggies, healthy fats. Leave 10-20% of calories for discretionary items if macros and fiber (10-15 g/1,000 kcal) are met. Plan treats around training or social events. Track trends (weight, performance) and adjust macros in small steps.

:::checklist
- Targets | Hit calories and macros daily; protein is priority.
- Quality | Keep **80-90%** whole-food choices; meet fiber targets.
- Flex buffer | Use **10-20%** calories for discretionary foods without displacing protein/micros.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Using “flexible” to justify nutrient-poor choices that crowd out protein and fiber.
- Ignoring fiber and micros, leading to digestion or performance issues.
- Large swings in daily intake that obscure progress trends.
