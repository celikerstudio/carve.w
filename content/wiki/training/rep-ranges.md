## What are rep ranges?
Rep ranges describe how many reps you perform per set to target strength, hypertrophy, or endurance adaptations. Different ranges shift the balance between load, tension, and fatigue, but all can build muscle when effort is high.

:::takeaway
Use **3-6 reps** for strength focus, **6-15 reps** for hypertrophy, and keep sets **1-3 RIR** to manage fatigue and drive [[progressive-overload]].
:::

## How does it work?
Lower reps with heavier loads emphasize neural adaptations and high-tension reps. Moderate reps blend tension and metabolic stress for [[hypertrophy]]. Higher reps increase metabolic fatigue but still grow muscle if taken near failure. Effort, not just reps, determines fiber recruitment, while total quality [[volume]] must stay recoverable.
Range selection also influences bar speed and skill practice: lower reps sharpen bracing and coordination under heavy load, while moderate reps provide more hypertrophy-friendly time under tension. Shifting ranges across blocks can resensitize progress without overhauling exercises.

## Practical application
Center most hypertrophy work at 6-15 reps with 1-3 reps in reserve. Use 3-6 rep top sets for strength, backed by moderate-rep back-offs. For accessories, 8-20 reps often balance stimulus and joint friendliness. Track RPE to prevent drift, and adjust reps before adding load if execution slips. Pair ranges with adequate [[protein]] and [[sleep]] to recover.
Over a mesocycle, you can wave rep ranges: slightly lower reps in weeks with heavier loads, then slightly higher reps as fatigue climbs to keep effort high without chasing maximal loads. For lifts with long ranges of motion, consider mid to higher reps to reduce joint stress while keeping tension high.

:::checklist
- Strength slots | 1-3 top sets at **3-6 reps** for force and skill.
- Hypertrophy base | Most work **6-15 reps**, **1-3 RIR** for quality volume.
- Accessories | Use **8-20 reps** to load muscles while sparing joints and fatigue.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Treating high reps as easy and stopping far from failure, wasting sets.
- Camping only in low reps, limiting [[volume]] and joint recovery.
- Ignoring RPE drift, letting form degrade as fatigue rises.
