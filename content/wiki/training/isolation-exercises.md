## What are isolation exercises?

An isolation exercise moves one joint and mainly trains one muscle group. A biceps curl moves the elbow, a leg extension moves the knee, and a lateral raise moves the shoulder. Researchers call them single-joint exercises. The opposite is a multi-joint exercise such as a squat or a row, which gets its own article: [[compound-exercises]].

:::takeaway
You do not have to do isolation exercises, but they have a place. The ACSM's 2009 position stand recommended **both single- and multi-joint exercises** for muscle growth [1], and its 2026 update, which replaced it, found **too few data** to say which type builds more muscle [2]. One meta-analysis found **similar growth** with either type [4]. Isolation exercises **target a muscle** and need **less skill** [1].
:::

## Should you do isolation exercises?

For muscle growth, the ACSM's 2009 position stand for healthy adults recommended including single- and multi-joint exercises, with free weights and with machines, for beginners, intermediate and advanced lifters. It rated this evidence category A, the highest of its categories [1]. The 2026 position stand replaced it. That one looked at 137 systematic reviews and found insufficient data to determine whether muscle growth differs between single-joint and multi-joint training [2]. So the current position stand gives no preference either way.

A 2021 position stand of experts, written for athletes, says programs should combine multi- and single-joint exercises to develop the whole muscle, with a variety of angles and planes of movement to stimulate the muscle completely [3]. For the studies on the arms and legs, see the next section and [[compound-exercises]].

For strength, the ACSM (2009) put the emphasis on multi-joint exercises [1].

## Do isolation exercises build more muscle than compound exercises?

In the studies that compared them, not clearly. A meta-analysis of 7 randomized trials on arm and leg muscles found similar growth of the whole muscle with single-joint and multi-joint exercises. Its authors suggest that combining both may help to target parts of a muscle, but add that this has hardly been studied [4]. Looking across reviews, the ACSM in 2026 found too few data to decide [2]. So an isolation exercise is a way to add work for a muscle that your compound lifts barely reach, not something you must have.

## Why do isolation exercises need less technique?

The ACSM describes single-joint exercises, such as knee extensions and knee curls, as a way to target specific muscle groups that asks for less skill and technical involvement [1]. Each one also trains fewer muscles at a time than a squat does.

## Where in the workout?

For strength, do the lifts you most want to improve early: in a meta-analysis of 11 studies, strength went up most in the exercises done at the start of a session [5]. For muscle growth the order made no difference in that meta-analysis [5], and the ACSM's 2026 review found the same for exercise order within a session [2]. In 2009 the ACSM recommended the same order for muscle growth as for strength, with the large muscle groups and multi-joint exercises first, and rated that evidence category C [1]. Putting isolation exercises after your compound lifts is therefore a sensible default, but for muscle growth it is not a requirement.

## How long should you rest between sets?

The ACSM's 2009 position stand recommended 1 to 2 minutes of rest between sets for novice and intermediate lifters training for muscle growth. For advanced lifters it suggested 2 to 3 minutes for heavy core exercises and 1 to 2 minutes for other exercises of moderate to moderately high intensity [1].

The 2021 expert position stand for athletes says rest should be at least 2 minutes for multi-joint exercises, and that 60 to 90 seconds can be used for single-joint and certain machine exercises [3]. If you are short on time, isolation exercises are where you can rest less.

For how many sets and reps, see [[volume]] and [[rep-ranges]].

## Common mistakes

- **Thinking you must do isolation work.** Whole-muscle growth was similar with either type in the studies so far [4]. It is useful for adding work to a muscle your compound lifts miss.
- **Saying isolation exercises are better, or worse.** The ACSM found too few data to decide [2].
