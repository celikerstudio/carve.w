## What are compound exercises?

A compound exercise moves more than one joint at a time, so several muscle groups work together. A squat bends your hips, knees and ankles and trains your thighs and glutes in one movement. Researchers call these multi-joint exercises.

An isolation exercise moves one joint and mainly trains one muscle group, like a biceps curl or a leg extension. These are single-joint exercises. See [[isolation-exercises]].

Common compound exercises:

- **Lower body** - squat, leg press, lunge, deadlift, Romanian deadlift.
- **Pushing** - bench press, push-up, overhead press, dips.
- **Pulling** - rows, pull-ups, lat pulldown.

:::takeaway
Do **compound exercises before isolation exercises**, and large muscle groups before small ones, as the ACSM advises [1]. Strength went up most in the exercises done **first** in a session [2]. For muscle growth in the arms and legs, compound and isolation exercises gave **similar results** [7], and the order made **no difference** [2].
:::

## Why do compound exercises go first?

The ACSM position stand recommends ordering a workout so you can keep the intensity up: large muscle groups before small ones, multi-joint exercises before single-joint ones, and more intense exercises before less intense ones [1]. The exercises that ask the most of you come while you are still fresh.

A 2021 meta-analysis of 11 studies tested this. Strength in compound exercises went up more when they came first. Strength in isolation exercises went up more when those came first. Muscle growth was similar with either order [2]. In short: whatever you want to get strongest at, do it early in the session.

## Are compound exercises better than isolation exercises?

For strength and fitness, they had the edge in one trial. Young amateur soccer players with no strength training experience trained for 8 weeks, after 4 weeks of getting used to the exercises, with either only compound or only isolation exercises. To keep the total work equal, the compound group lifted heavier weights for 6 to 8 reps per set and the isolation group lighter weights for 12 to 18 reps. The compound group gained more on the bench press, squat and leg extension, and improved their aerobic fitness more. Body fat and lean mass changed the same in both groups. The authors point to the difference in reps as one possible explanation for the strength results, and note that the players' soccer training may have affected the outcome [5].

For muscle growth, the studies found no clear winner. In untrained young men, 10 weeks of lat pulldowns grew the elbow flexors about as much as 10 weeks of biceps curls [4]. A 2017 review found that long-term studies of the upper limbs showed no difference in size and strength between the two types, and no extra gain from adding isolation exercises to a compound program [3]. A 2023 meta-analysis of 7 studies on the muscles of the arms and legs found similar growth of the whole muscle with either type. Its authors suggest that combining both may help to grow specific parts of a muscle, but add that this has hardly been studied [7].

The practical reason to build around compound exercises is time. One squat trains your thighs and glutes in a single movement, while covering the same muscles with isolation exercises takes several. The 2017 review was written with that in mind: lack of time is a common reason people do not exercise, and for the upper limbs adding isolation work gave nothing extra [3]. Compound exercises make it easier to train every major muscle group, which the WHO advises on 2 or more days a week [6].

## Do you need isolation exercises?

You do not have to, but they have a place. The ACSM includes both single- and multi-joint exercises in strength programs [1]. The 2017 review concluded that isolation work may be useful for the lower back muscles and for correcting imbalances between muscles [3].

If a muscle you care about gets little work from your compound exercises, an isolation exercise is a simple way to add sets for it. Put them after your compound work, so they do not take away from your heavier lifts [1][2].

## How do you build a workout around compound exercises?

Pick one or two compound exercises for each part of the body, and add isolation work after them if you have time.

- **Start** with a lower body compound, such as a squat or leg press.
- **Then** a push and a pull, such as a bench press and a row.
- **Finish** with isolation exercises for the muscles you want more of, such as curls, lateral raises or calf raises.

Over the week, make sure every major muscle group gets trained [6]. How often to train each muscle is covered in [[frequency]], how many sets in [[volume]].

:::checklist
- Order | Compound exercises first, isolation exercises last [1].
- Priority | Do the lift you most want to improve early in the session [2].
- Coverage | Train every major muscle group over the week [6].
- Extras | Add isolation work for muscles your compound exercises miss.
:::

## Common mistakes

- **Doing isolation work first when you want a stronger squat or bench.** Strength went up most in the exercises done first [2].
- **Thinking isolation exercises are useless.** For muscle growth in the arms and legs they did as well as compound exercises [7].
- **Rushing heavy compound lifts with poor form.** These are the exercises with the most weight on the bar. Learn the movement with a lighter weight first. See [[warm-up]] and [[injury-prevention]].
