## What are body types?
Body type labels (ectomorph, mesomorph, endomorph) are informal ways to describe natural tendencies in size or leanness. They can guide expectations but should not limit training and nutrition choices.

:::takeaway
Traits vary individually; tailor **calories, protein, and training** to your response, not a fixed label.
:::

## How does it work?
Genetics influence limb lengths, appetite, and how easily you gain fat or muscle. Labels are oversimplified; most people are mixed. Personalized programming—managing [[calories]], [[protein]], and [[progressive-overload]]—matters more.

## Practical application
If you stay lean easily (ectomorph-leaning), emphasize calorie-dense foods to hit a surplus and keep [[volume]] recoverable. If you gain fat easily (endomorph-leaning), track portions, prioritize satiety (protein/fiber), and keep steps high. Everyone benefits from lifting, adequate protein, and sleep. Adjust based on results, not labels.

:::checklist
- Nutrition | Match calories to your response; protein **1.6-2.2 g/kg** for all.
- Training | Use structured overload; adjust volume to recovery capacity.
- Habits | Sleep 7-9h, monitor trends, and tweak calmly.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Using labels to excuse poor adherence.
- Copying “ecto/endo” meal plans without tracking results.
- Ignoring that strength, protein, and sleep help everyone.
