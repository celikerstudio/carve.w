## What is volume?
Volume is the total work performed, usually tracked as sets x reps x load per muscle group. It captures how much mechanical tension and metabolic stress you apply over a session or week. Alongside [[intensity]] and recovery, well-managed volume is the primary driver of [[hypertrophy]].

:::takeaway
Aim for **10-20** hard sets per muscle per week, split over **2-3** sessions, with **1-3 RIR** on most sets.
:::

## How does it work?
Each quality set near failure triggers muscle protein synthesis, and repeated bouts accumulate adaptation. Research shows a dose-response up to an individual ceiling; beyond that, junk volume adds fatigue without extra growth. Volume must progress gradually with [[progressive-overload]] so workload climbs as you adapt, while recovery capacity keeps pace.

## Practical application
Track hard sets per muscle and per movement pattern. For hypertrophy, use mostly 6-15 reps at RPE 7-9, leaving 1-3 reps in reserve. Increase volume slowly—add 1-2 sets per muscle per week across a block if performance and sleep stay solid. Pair higher volume phases with adequate [[protein]] and [[calories]] and insert a deload every 6-10 weeks or when performance trends down. Spread sets across the week to improve skill practice and manage soreness.
Log session RPE, soreness, and sleep quality to confirm the workload is productive rather than simply exhausting.

:::checklist
- Weekly sets | Keep **10-20** hard sets per muscle per week; start low and add only when recovering well.
- Effort target | Hold most sets at RPE 7-9 (1-3 RIR) to balance stimulus and fatigue.
- Deload cadence | Deload every **6-10** weeks or sooner if bar speed, mood, or sleep decline.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Chasing more sets instead of improving execution and effort quality, creating junk volume.
- Ignoring recovery markers and letting fatigue mask progress.
- Skipping deloads, which raises overuse risk and flattens long-term gains.
