## What are drop sets?
Drop sets extend a set by reducing load after reaching near-failure, then continuing for more reps. They add metabolic stress and time under tension to drive [[hypertrophy]] without long sessions.

:::takeaway
Use **1 drop** of **10-25% load** on the last set of an exercise, keeping total drops **1-2** to avoid excessive fatigue.
:::

## How does it work?
After the initial near-failure set, lowering load keeps fibers working under fatigue, increasing metabolic stress and recruitment. Because drop sets spike local fatigue, they should be used sparingly to avoid cutting into overall weekly [[volume]] quality.

## Practical application
Reserve drop sets for accessories (curls, laterals, leg extensions), not heavy compounds. Perform a top set at ~1-2 RIR, drop 10-25% load, and continue to near-failure once or twice. Track weekly fatigue; if main lifts suffer, reduce drops. Ensure form stays tight despite burning muscles.

:::checklist
- Placement | Use on accessories; avoid heavy spine-loaded compounds.
- Dose | **1-2 drops** of **10-25%**; finish near failure with control.
- Monitor | If performance on main lifts dips, reduce or skip drop sets.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Turning every set into multiple drops, wrecking recovery.
- Using drop sets on technical lifts, risking form breakdown.
- Letting sloppiness creep in as fatigue rises, reducing stimulus quality.
