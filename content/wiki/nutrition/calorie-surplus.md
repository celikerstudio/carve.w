## What is a calorie surplus?
A calorie surplus means eating more calories than you expend, providing extra energy and substrate for muscle gain. When paired with progressive training, it supports faster [[hypertrophy]] than maintenance intakes.

:::takeaway
Use a moderate surplus of **150-300 kcal/day** to gain roughly **0.25-0.5%** of body weight per week while limiting fat gain.
:::

## How does it work?
Surplus calories increase energy availability, improving training performance, recovery, and muscle protein synthesis. Excessive surplus raises fat gain without meaningfully speeding muscle growth. Adequate [[protein]] and carbs improve how the surplus is used, while smart [[volume]] and [[progressive-overload]] direct the extra energy toward muscle.

## Practical application
Set protein at 1.6-2.2 g/kg and fats at 0.6-1.0 g/kg; fill the rest with carbs to fuel training. Track weekly weight and strength; aim for 0.25-0.5% body weight gain per week. If weight jumps faster, reduce 100-200 kcal; if stagnant for 2 weeks, add 100-150 kcal. Keep steps and sleep consistent to interpret trends. Favor whole foods but use calorie-dense options (rice, oats, nut butters) to meet targets without digestive overload.
Build in guardrails: cap fat gain by pausing to a 1-2 week maintenance if gains outpace strength. Consider short 2-4 week mini-cuts after longer surplus phases to tidy up without losing momentum.

:::checklist
- Surplus size | Start with **+150-300 kcal/day**; adjust using weekly weight trends.
- Protein anchor | Hold **1.6-2.2 g/kg** protein to bias gains toward muscle.
- Performance check | Gains should pair with steady load/reps increases; if not, adjust training or surplus.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Pushing huge surpluses that add mostly fat.
- Letting activity drop, masking whether the surplus is appropriate.
- Ignoring digestion; overeating low-density foods can cause discomfort and missed targets.
