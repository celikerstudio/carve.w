## What is training volume?

Volume is how much work you do. In this article it means the number of hard sets you do for a muscle group in a week. Ten sets of squats, leg press and lunges across the week is ten sets for your quadriceps. A set that mainly trains another muscle counts for less, see below. The ACSM asks for high effort in a set, see [[intensity]].

Volume is one of the main things you can change in a program, next to how heavy you lift and how often you train. See [[frequency]] for how to spread it over the week.

:::takeaway
Do **at least 2 sets per exercise** [1]. In the reviews the ACSM analysed, muscle growth was higher with **10 or more sets per muscle group per week** [1]. From about **18 to 20 weekly sets** each extra set added less, and the exact best number cannot be pinned down [1].
:::

## How many sets per exercise?

The ACSM position stand of 2026 is an overview of 137 systematic reviews in healthy adults, from complete novices to experienced trainees. It replaces the 2009 position stand [1]. Its authors write that one set is clearly better than none and that two sets are better than one. They also write that the exact number of sets needed to get the most out of training cannot be ascertained [1]. For strength the gains levelled off beyond roughly 2 to 3 sets per exercise, so healthy adults are advised to do at least two sets per exercise [1]. A third set or more may add something, but the authors expect each extra set to add less [1].

## How many sets per muscle per week?

For muscle growth, the same overview found more growth with higher volume, defined as 10 or more sets per muscle group per week [1]. It also states that the dose-response for growth "plateaued and showed diminishing returns" at about 18 to 20 weekly sets. That figure comes from a meta-regression by Pelland and colleagues, which the ACSM cites [1][4].

How Pelland counted matters for your own numbers. They counted sets per muscle group, and a set that trains a muscle only indirectly, such as the bench press for the triceps, counted as half a set [4]. Ten triceps sets plus ten bench press sets would count as fifteen triceps sets.

The paper is more careful than the ACSM sentence. It combined 67 studies (2,058 participants, 79% men, average age 25) and found that both muscle size and strength rose with weekly sets, with diminishing returns that were considerably more pronounced for strength [4]. In the preprint version, strength showed strong diminishing returns and a functional plateau, while for muscle growth the authors found no clear plateau and noted more uncertainty at higher volumes, as few studies had gone beyond about 25 weekly sets [4].

An expert consensus for athletes sees about 10 sets per muscle per week as a general minimum to maximize growth. Some people grow well on a bit less, and higher volumes may help muscles that lag behind [3].

All of this comes from groups of people. Use the numbers as a starting point and adjust to how you recover.

## How many sets should a beginner start with?

At least two sets per exercise, like everyone else. The 2026 position stand applies from complete novices to experienced trainees, and much of its evidence comes from people with little or no training experience [1].

The 2009 position stand, which the 2026 one replaces, was more cautious: it recommended that novices start with one to three sets per exercise [2]. The 2026 advice drops the option of a single set. See [[rep-ranges]] for the reps that go with it.

## How fast can you add sets?

Add them gradually. The expert consensus for athletes says it may be prudent to raise the number of sets for a muscle by no more than 20% of the previous volume per training cycle of about four weeks, and then reassess [3]. For ten sets, that is two extra sets over about a month.

That advice is written for athletes who want to maximize muscle growth. It is a cautious suggestion from experts, not a measured limit. For ways other than adding sets to make training harder, see [[progressive-overload]].

## When do you need a lighter week?

Adding sets slowly is one way to keep volume manageable. A planned lighter week is another, see [[deload]] for what is known about it.

:::checklist
- Per exercise | Do at least 2 sets [1].
- Per week | Count your sets per muscle. Growth was higher from about 10 a week [1].
- Starting out | Two sets per exercise is the minimum, also for beginners [1]. The 2026 statement replaces the 2009 advice of one to three sets [1][2].
- Adding | Add a few sets at a time, for athletes at most about 20% per four weeks [3].
:::

## Common mistakes

- **Treating 18 to 20 sets as a target.** The ACSM describes diminishing returns beyond that point; it is not a goal [1].
- **Adding many sets at once.** The athlete consensus suggests adding at most about 20% per four weeks [3].
- **Doing sets that are far from hard.** The ACSM asks for high effort [1]. See [[intensity]].
- **Expecting more sets to keep adding strength.** The gain per extra set flattened much faster for strength than for muscle size [4].
