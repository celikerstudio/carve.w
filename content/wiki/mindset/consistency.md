## What is consistency?
Consistency is following your training, nutrition, and recovery plan regularly over time. It compounds small actions into significant results. For most lifters, steady execution matters more than perfect programming or equipment.

:::takeaway
Protect a **3-5 day** weekly schedule, keep **simple meal defaults**, and aim for **1-3 RIR** on main lifts each session.
:::

## How does it work?
Habits reduce friction by making desired actions automatic. Clear cues and routines help you start sessions even when motivation is low. Consistency enables progressive training blocks, steady [[protein]] intake, and reliable [[sleep]] schedules, which together drive adaptation. It also yields clean data to adjust [[calories]], [[volume]], or [[intensity]] when progress stalls.
Implementation intentions (if-then plans) connect triggers to actions—like lifting right after work—which shrink the effort needed to get started.

## Practical application
Set a realistic weekly template and keep it stable. Anchor workouts to fixed times, prep clothes and snacks the day before, and maintain a short list of default meals that hit protein and calorie targets. Track simple weekly metrics—weights lifted, body weight, steps—to stay engaged without obsession. When life gets busy, shorten sessions instead of skipping entirely to keep the habit loop intact.
Reduce decision fatigue by planning the first two lifts of every session in advance and pre-logging meals where possible. Use reminders or a partner for accountability during stressful weeks.

:::checklist
- Schedule | Commit to **3-5** training days; lock times on the calendar.
- Meal defaults | Keep 3-5 go-to meals that hit [[protein]] and calorie needs with minimal prep.
- Minimum viable session | When busy, run a shortened session to protect the habit and data flow.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Chasing novelty over adherence, swapping programs before adaptations occur.
- Setting goals beyond available time or recovery, leading to repeated resets.
- Ignoring small wins and feedback, which erodes motivation and adherence.
