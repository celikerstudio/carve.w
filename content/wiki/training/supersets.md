## What are supersets?
Supersets pair two exercises back-to-back with minimal rest, either for opposing muscles (e.g., push/pull) or the same muscle. They increase training density and can save time while maintaining stimulus.

:::takeaway
Pair **non-competing** movements for efficiency; keep loads and RIR consistent so quality and technique stay high.
:::

## How does it work?
Alternating muscles lets one recover while the other works, preserving performance. Supersetting the same muscle increases local fatigue and pump but may reduce load. Density rises, so cardiovascular demand increases slightly. Good pairing maintains form and keeps [[volume]] effective without wrecking main lifts.

## Practical application
Use antagonistic pairs (row + press, curl + triceps) for best performance. Keep supersets mostly on accessories, not your heaviest compounds. Rest 60-120s after the pair. Track RPE per exercise to prevent drift. For same-muscle supersets, lower load slightly and maintain 1-2 RIR to protect form.

:::checklist
- Pairing | Favor push/pull or upper/lower accessory pairs to avoid interference.
- Load | Keep loads appropriate; drop slightly if form slips under density.
- Placement | Use on accessories; keep main strength lifts unsupersetted.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Supersetting heavy compounds, tanking performance and technique.
- Letting RIR unknowingly drop because rest is too short.
- Pairing movements that fatigue the same stabilizers, causing form breakdown.
