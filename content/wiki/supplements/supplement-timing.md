## What is supplement timing?
Supplement timing is scheduling products when they work best or are easiest to remember. It improves consistency and minimizes side effects.

:::takeaway
Time **caffeine/pre-workout** pre-lift, **creatine** anytime daily, **protein** around meals, **electrolytes** for long/hot sessions; consistency beats precision for most.
:::

## How does it work?
Some supplements depend on acute timing (caffeine ~30-60 min pre). Others, like [[creatine]], rely on saturation and just need daily dosing. Aligning intake with digestion and sleep reduces GI issues and insomnia. Habit anchors improve adherence.

## Practical application
Create a simple schedule: morning/midday for stimulants; post-lift or with meals for creatine and protein; bedtime for magnesium if calming; vitamin D with a fat-containing meal. For beta-alanine, split doses; for melatonin, 30-60 min pre-bed. Track sleep and GI response and adjust timing accordingly.

:::checklist
- Stims | **30-60 min** pre-lift; avoid late to protect sleep.
- Daily staples | Take creatine and fish oil with meals; timing flexible.
- Calm supports | Magnesium/melatonin later if they help relaxation.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Taking stimulants late and harming sleep.
- Inconsistent dosing (creatine, beta-alanine), preventing saturation.
- Overcomplicating schedules, reducing adherence.
