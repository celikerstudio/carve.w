## What is omega-3?

EPA and DHA are the omega-3 fatty acids in fish. They also come in fish oil and algae oil capsules [2]. This article is about EPA and DHA, not about ALA, the omega-3 fat from plants. It covers what the Dutch nutrition centre (Voedingscentrum, which cites the Gezondheidsraad, the Dutch Health Council) and the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) say about fish, capsules and safety.

:::takeaway
The Voedingscentrum says the Gezondheidsraad advises adults **200 mg** of omega-3 from fish a day, and that one fish meal a week, preferably oily fish, gives you enough [1]. It is not clear enough whether capsules do the same as fish [2]. High-dose EPA and DHA supplements can raise the risk of heart rhythm disorders, most at **4 g a day** [1].
:::

## How much omega-3 do you need?

The Voedingscentrum says the Gezondheidsraad advises adults 200 mg of omega-3 fatty acids from fish a day. If you eat fish once a week, you get enough, and it says to choose oily fish such as herring or salmon [1]. A second Voedingscentrum page gives the advice as 100 g of sustainable fish a week, preferably oily fish [2]. If you have heart disease, the advice is a little more fish, 1 to 2 times a week [1].

One portion of fish a week goes with a lower chance of dying from heart disease [1]. The Voedingscentrum's fish page says eating fish lowers the chance of coronary heart disease, mostly oily fish such as salmon, rainbow trout and herring [2].

EFSA has its own reference value: an adequate intake of 250 mg a day of EPA and DHA for maintaining general cardiovascular health in healthy adults and children [4]. That is a little above the Dutch 200 mg, and the sources do not say why the two differ. (The abstract of EFSA's safety opinion also mentions dietary recommendations of 250 to 500 mg a day for European adults. EFSA's own figure is the 250 mg above [3][4].)

## Do omega-3 capsules work as well as fish?

That is not known well enough. EPA and DHA can come from a fish oil or algae oil capsule, but there is not enough research to say whether the capsule gives the same health effects as eating fish [2].

There is some research on supplements. The Voedingscentrum reports that 1 g of EPA and DHA a day lowered the risk of dying from heart disease by about 10%, in people with (or at high risk of) cardiovascular disease [1]. The page gives that result for that group only.

## Is a high dose of omega-3 safe?

High doses carry a warning. The Voedingscentrum says supplements with high doses of EPA and DHA can raise the risk of heart rhythm disorders, and that this risk is largest at 4 g a day. It says that if you choose a supplement, you should not take more than the recommended amount [1]. The page does not say what amount it means.

EFSA looked at safety in 2012 and could not set an upper limit, because the data were not enough for any population group [3]. It did find that supplements of up to 5 g a day of EPA and DHA combined (or 1.8 g a day of EPA alone) do not raise safety concerns for adults, and that long-term intakes of up to about 5 g a day did not appear to raise the risk of bleeding, as long as the oils have not oxidised [3][4]. For DHA alone the figure is about 1 g a day for the general population [3].

The two sources do not draw the line in the same place. The EFSA opinion is from 2012 [3], and the Voedingscentrum page shows no date [1]. The sources do not say which findings the Voedingscentrum warning rests on. The Voedingscentrum warning is the more cautious of the two, and EFSA's figure is where it saw no safety concerns, not a dose to aim for.

## Who should be careful with omega-3 supplements?

- **Blood thinners.** There are signs that from a few grams of DHA and/or EPA a day, blood clots more slowly. If you use blood thinners and take more than 1 g of EPA and DHA a day, discuss it with your doctor [1].
- **High doses in general.** They can raise the risk of heart rhythm disorders, most at 4 g a day [1].
- **Cholesterol.** 2 g of DHA a day raises LDL cholesterol by about 0.20 mmol/l [1]. EFSA found that 2 to 6 g a day of EPA and DHA combined raises LDL by about 3%, which it says may not have an adverse effect on cardiovascular disease risk [3].

## Is there a supplement advice for pregnancy?

Yes. On the page used here, it is the only group for which the Voedingscentrum names an omega-3 supplement. The advice in pregnancy is to eat fish twice a week, once oily and once lean. If you do not want fish or cannot manage twice a week, the advice is to take a daily omega-3 supplement with 250 to 450 mg DHA throughout the pregnancy [1].

## Does omega-3 help muscle, recovery or fat loss?

The sources behind this article look at heart health and safety. They do not cover muscle growth, recovery or fat loss, so this article says nothing about those. The Voedingscentrum says there is not enough evidence that omega-3 supplements help with learning problems, ADHD, depression or dementia [1].

:::checklist
- Fish | The Voedingscentrum: one fish meal a week, preferably oily fish like herring or salmon [1].
- Capsules | Not clear enough whether they do the same as fish [2].
- High doses | Can raise the risk of heart rhythm disorders, most at **4 g** a day [1].
- Blood thinners | With more than 1 g EPA and DHA a day, talk to your doctor [1].
:::

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming a capsule is the same as a fish meal.** There is not enough research to say [2].
- **Reading EFSA's 5 g as a dose.** It is the level up to which EFSA saw no safety concerns in 2012, and the Voedingscentrum warns about heart rhythm disorders at high doses, most at 4 g [1][3].
- **Taking more than 1 g a day on blood thinners without talking to a doctor.** There are signs that blood clots more slowly at higher doses [1].
- **Expecting a result for muscle or fat loss.** The sources here do not show one.
