## What should you eat after a workout?

After training, your muscles repair and build new protein. If the session was long and hard, they also refill their store of carbohydrate, called glycogen. What you eat in the hours afterwards supports both. For most people who lift, a normal meal with a good portion of protein does the job.

:::takeaway
Eat a meal or snack with **20 to 40 g of protein** in the hours after training [1]. There is no one-hour panic window: your muscles stay more responsive to protein for up to 24 hours after a hard session [2][3]. Carbs straight after training matter mainly when you train again within a few hours [1].
:::

## Is there an anabolic window after training?

Not a narrow one. The ISSN, a sports nutrition society, says that protein eaten from right after training up to two hours later strongly raises muscle protein building [1]. But the effect of a workout lasts much longer than that. The ISSN's protein position stand says muscle is sensitized to protein for up to 24 hours after resistance exercise, though the effect probably fades as the hours pass [2]. In a study of 15 young men, a 15 g whey drink raised muscle protein synthesis (the building of new muscle fibre protein) more 24 hours after leg exercise to failure than the same drink at rest [3].

Training studies point the same way. A meta-analysis pooled 23 trials on muscle growth and 20 on strength. In a simple pooled analysis, protein around a workout had a small to moderate effect on muscle growth and none on strength. Once total daily protein and other factors were taken into account, the effect on growth disappeared, and total protein was the strongest predictor of muscle growth [4]. A later review of 65 trials found that extra protein increased lean mass in adults, with no benefit from any particular timing [10].

A 2025 meta-analysis took only the five trials that directly compared protein before with protein after training. Two were in young people who trained, three in older untrained people, one of them with type 2 diabetes. Lean mass gains did not differ. Leg press strength improved a little more with protein before training, a borderline result the authors say needs more research [5]. In one of those trials, 21 trained men took 25 g of protein either right before or right after training for 10 weeks, and both groups gained similar strength and muscle. The groups were small, 9 and 12 men, and both ended up eating less than planned [6].

The ISSN's practical advice is to eat as soon as you can after a workout, because skipping protein afterwards brings no benefit and a meal may help or do nothing [1][2]. If that does not fit your day and dinner is an hour or two away, the studies above suggest waiting for dinner is fine. See [[meal-timing]] for timing across the whole day.

## How much protein should you eat after a workout?

The ISSN advises 20 to 40 g of a high-quality protein, which is 0.25 to 0.40 g per kg of bodyweight, with about 10 to 12 g of essential amino acids in it [1]. For an 80 kg person that is 20 to 32 g. Meat, fish, eggs, dairy, soy and whey all get you there; see [[protein-sources]].

After a workout that trains your whole body, the higher end may be worth it. In young men who lifted regularly, 40 g of whey after a whole-body session raised muscle protein building more than 20 g [9]. For your daily total, see [[protein]].

## Do you need carbs after lifting?

Not for your muscles to grow. In a study of nine young men, adding 50 g of carbohydrate to 25 g of whey after leg training did not raise muscle protein building any further [7]. The ISSN suggests that carbs after a workout likely add little for muscle as long as you eat enough protein [1].

Carbs do matter for refilling glycogen. In 12 male cyclists, carbohydrate taken right after a long ride refilled muscle glycogen faster than the same amount taken two hours later [8]. Lifting uses less glycogen than that kind of endurance work, and truly running out of glycogen from lifting is unlikely [11]. It is not nothing, though: six sets of leg extensions lowered glycogen in the trained muscle by 39% [1].

According to the ISSN, when you eat carbs after training matters most in two cases: when you need your glycogen back within about four hours, and when you are not eating enough carbohydrate overall [1]. Otherwise your daily total is what counts. In elite athletes training at high volumes, a diet with at least 8 g of carbohydrate per kg a day restored glycogen within 24 hours [1]. If you lift a few times a week and train once a day, a normal meal is enough. See [[carbohydrates]].

## What is a good meal after training?

Anything with a good portion of protein that you will actually eat. A chicken and rice dish, yogurt with fruit and oats, eggs on toast, or a shake with a banana all work. If you did a long or sweaty session, drink as well; see [[hydration]].

## Your checklist

:::checklist
- Protein | 20 to 40 g in the meal after training, within a couple of hours if it fits your day.
- Daily total | Your protein for the whole day matters more than the timing; see [[protein]].
- Carbs | A normal portion after lifting. More, and sooner, if you train again within a few hours.
- Fluids | After a sweaty session, drink a bit more than you lost; see [[hydration]].
:::
