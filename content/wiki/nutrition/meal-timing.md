## What is meal timing?
Meal timing is how you schedule food around the day and training. Good timing supports energy, training quality, and recovery while keeping adherence easy.

:::takeaway
Center **20-60 g carbs** and **20-40 g protein** in the **1-3 hours pre/post** training; keep total daily targets the priority.
:::

## How does it work?
Carbs near training improve glycogen availability and lower perceived effort. Protein pre/post provides amino acids for repair. Spacing meals steadies energy and appetite, aiding adherence. Timing is secondary to total [[calories]] and [[protein]], but it can add a performance edge.

## Practical application
Eat 3-5 meals/day. Pre-lift (1-3 hours): 20-60 g carbs, 20-40 g protein, low-moderate fat. Post-lift (0-3 hours): similar protein and carbs to refuel. Bookend sleep with protein (e.g., dairy/slow protein at night). Adjust meal size to your schedule; keep fiber modest pre-lift to avoid GI issues.

:::checklist
- Pre-lift | **20-60 g carbs + 20-40 g protein** 1-3 hours before.
- Post-lift | Repeat protein/carb focus within **0-3 hours**.
- Daily | 3-5 evenly spaced feedings to hit macros and manage hunger.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Training fasted with no carbs/protein despite hard sessions, hurting performance.
- Giant fat-heavy meals pre-lift causing sluggishness.
- Obsessing over minutes while ignoring daily macro targets.
