## What is progress tracking?
Progress tracking measures training, body composition, and habits to inform adjustments. It keeps you honest and data-driven.

:::takeaway
Track **key lifts**, **weekly body weight averages**, and **adherence** to protein/sessions; review **weekly**.
:::

## How does it work?
Consistent metrics reveal trends beyond day-to-day noise. Tracking lifts shows [[progressive-overload]] success; weight trends show calorie alignment; adherence logs expose habit gaps. Data guides small adjustments instead of guesswork.

## Practical application
Log loads/reps, RPE for main lifts; weigh daily or several times weekly, average it; track protein and sessions completed. Note sleep and stress weekly. Review on a set day; adjust calories by 100-200 kcal or training volume modestly if trends stall. Avoid obsessing over single bad days.

:::checklist
- Lifts | Record loads/reps/RPE on main movements.
- Body | Use **weekly averages** for weight; take periodic waist photos/measurements.
- Habits | Log protein intake and session completion; review weekly.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Reacting to single weigh-ins instead of averages.
- Tracking too many metrics and burning out.
- Making big changes without data.
