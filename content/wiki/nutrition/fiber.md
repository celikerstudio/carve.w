## What is fiber?
Dietary fiber is the indigestible part of plant foods that supports gut health, satiety, and blood sugar control. It aids digestion and long-term health while supporting diet adherence.

:::takeaway
Aim for **10-15 g fiber per 1,000 kcal** from fruits, veggies, legumes, and whole grains; adjust to tolerance.
:::

## How does it work?
Soluble fiber slows digestion and stabilizes glucose; insoluble adds bulk for regularity. Fiber feeds gut microbes, producing short-chain fatty acids that support gut lining and metabolic health. It increases fullness, helping maintain calorie targets during cuts.

## Practical application
Include vegetables at most meals, 2-3 fruits daily, and some whole grains/legumes. Increase fiber gradually and hydrate to avoid GI distress. Time high-fiber meals away from immediate pre-lift windows to prevent bloating. Track tolerance; some sources (beans, certain veggies) may require portion tweaks.

:::checklist
- Target | **10-15 g/1,000 kcal**; increase slowly.
- Sources | Mix fruit, vegetables, legumes, whole grains, nuts/seeds.
- Timing | Keep very high-fiber meals away from pre-lift if they cause discomfort.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Jumping fiber intake too fast, causing bloating.
- Ignoring water intake when increasing fiber.
- Over-relying on supplements instead of varied whole-food sources.
