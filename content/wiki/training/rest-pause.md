## What is rest-pause?
Rest-pause training breaks one set into mini-bouts separated by short rests (10-30s), allowing more high-quality reps near failure in less time.

:::takeaway
Use **1 top set** near failure, then **1-2 mini-bouts** after **10-30s** rests; limit to accessories or stable compounds you can control.
:::

## How does it work?
Short rests partially restore phosphocreatine, letting you squeeze extra near-failure reps at the same load. This raises intensity and fiber recruitment without long sessions. Because fatigue is high, placement and exercise choice matter to protect form.

## Practical application
Pick stable lifts (machines, hacks, presses with safeties). Do a near-failure set at 1-2 RIR, rest 10-30s, then add 2-5 reps; repeat once more if form holds. Keep total rest-pause exposures to 1-2 exercises per session. Track recovery; if next-day performance drops, reduce frequency.

:::checklist
- Exercise choice | Favor machines or supported lifts for safety.
- Structure | Top set **1-2 RIR**, rest **10-30s**, add **2-5 reps** for **1-2 bouts**.
- Control | Stop if form degrades; don’t use on technical free-weight compounds.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Using rest-pause on highly technical lifts, increasing injury risk.
- Overusing the method and crushing recovery for main lifts.
- Ignoring form under fatigue, turning quality reps into junk.
