## What is a calorie deficit?
A calorie deficit means consuming fewer calories than you expend, leading to body fat loss. Sustained, well-managed deficits reduce weight while preserving muscle when paired with resistance training and adequate protein.

:::takeaway
Aim for a deficit that loses **0.5-1.0% body weight/week**, with **1.6-2.2 g/kg** [[protein]] and steady training to maintain muscle.
:::

## How does it work?
Energy imbalance drives fat loss as the body mobilizes stored fuel. Protein and resistance training protect lean mass. Moderate deficits sustain performance, [[sleep]], and adherence better than aggressive cuts. Tracking helps align intake with activity and hunger signals.
Adequate carbs support training quality, which maintains strength and muscle. Regular meals and fiber help manage hunger and blood sugar, reducing binge risk. Consistency—same scale time, steady steps—keeps feedback clean so small adjustments work.

## Practical application
Estimate maintenance, then reduce 300-600 kcal/day to start. Set protein 1.6-2.2 g/kg; keep fats 0.6-0.9 g/kg for hormones; fill remaining calories with carbs to fuel [[volume]] and [[intensity]]. Monitor weekly weight averages, gym performance, and hunger. Adjust by 100-200 kcal if loss stalls for 2+ weeks. Prioritize fiber (10-15 g/1,000 kcal) and hydration for satiety.
Use meal timing that fits your schedule—e.g., larger meals after training to blunt hunger. Plan occasional higher-carb refeed days if adherence drops, but keep weekly deficit intact. Track steps to avoid subconscious activity drops that slow fat loss.

:::checklist
- Deficit size | Target **0.5-1.0%/week** loss; adjust in **100-200 kcal** steps.
- Muscle retention | Lift 2-4x/week and hit **1.6-2.2 g/kg** protein.
- Adherence | Use fiber, hydration, and meal timing to manage hunger.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Cutting calories too aggressively, tanking performance and muscle.
- Dropping carbs so low that training quality and mood suffer.
- Not tracking trends, reacting to single weigh-ins instead of weekly averages.
