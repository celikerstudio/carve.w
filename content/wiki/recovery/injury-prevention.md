## What is injury prevention?
Injury prevention combines smart programming, technique, and recovery habits to reduce the likelihood of training-related setbacks. It keeps you consistent and progressing over the long term.

:::takeaway
Prioritize **technique**, gradual load progress, and **7-9 hours** of [[sleep]]; address weak links with accessories and mobility where needed.
:::

## How does it work?
Most lifting injuries stem from overload relative to tissue tolerance, poor movement patterns, or inadequate recovery. Managing [[volume]] and [[intensity]], refining form, and maintaining strength balance reduce joint stress. Recovery (sleep, nutrition) supports tissue repair and resilience.
Good sleep and protein intake improve collagen turnover and connective tissue health, while gradual exposure builds tolerance. Monitoring niggles early keeps small issues from turning chronic.

## Practical application
Use structured programs with planned progressions and deloads. Keep 1-3 RIR on most sets; avoid frequent maxing. Warm up with ramp-up sets and movement prep for tight areas. Include accessories for stability (e.g., upper back, hamstrings, rotator cuff) and mobility where range is limiting. Track pain signals; if discomfort persists, reduce load/volume and address form before returning to heavier work.
Vary grips and stances across cycles to distribute stress. If a pattern repeatedly irritates a joint, swap to a close variation (e.g., neutral-grip press) while addressing the cause. Keep daily movement (steps, light mobility) to maintain tissue quality without adding fatigue.
If pain lingers, seek assessment and use graded exposure back to prior loads. Layer recovery basics—[[sleep]] 7-9 hours, adequate [[protein]], and hydration—to support tissue repair.

:::checklist
- Load management | Progress gradually, keep **1-3 RIR**, and deload regularly.
- Technique & prep | Warm up, film key lifts, and reinforce bracing and alignment.
- Balances | Train weak links and stabilizers; adjust if pain or niggles persist.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Rapid load jumps without allowing tissues to adapt.
- Ignoring technique drift and discomfort until it becomes pain.
- Skipping accessories that support joints and stability around big lifts.
