## What is Progressive Overload?

Progressive overload means gradually making your training harder over time. This is **the most important principle** for muscle growth and strength gains.

:::takeaway
Without progressive overload, your muscles have no reason to grow. If you're doing the same weight and reps for months, you're maintaining—not building.
:::

## How does it work?

Your muscles adapt to stress. If you always do the same thing, they stop growing. By increasing the load, you force them to get stronger.

## Ways to progress

:::checklist
- More weight | The most direct form—add 1-2.5kg when you hit your rep target.
- More reps | Go from 8 to 10 reps with the same weight.
- More sets | Add an extra set to increase total volume.
- Better technique | More range of motion, slower negatives.
:::

## Practical example

Week 1: Bench press 60kg × 8 reps
Week 2: Bench press 60kg × 9 reps
Week 3: Bench press 60kg × 10 reps
Week 4: Bench press 62.5kg × 8 reps

## Common mistakes

- **Expecting daily progress** - Focus on weeks and months, not days
- **Ego lifting** - Adding weight at the cost of form defeats the purpose
- **Ignoring other variables** - Reps, sets, and technique count too
