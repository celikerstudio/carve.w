## How does alcohol affect fitness?
Alcohol adds empty calories, impairs recovery, and can disrupt [[sleep]]. Moderate intake can fit, but heavy drinking slows strength, [[hypertrophy]], and fat loss.

:::takeaway
Limit to **0-1 drink/day** on training days; prioritize protein, hydration, and sleep when alcohol is consumed.
:::

## How does it work?
Alcohol diverts metabolism toward clearing ethanol, pausing fat oxidation. It can lower muscle protein synthesis after training and reduce sleep quality, hindering recovery. Liquid calories add up quickly and loosen dietary restraint, risking calorie overages.

## Practical application
If drinking, do so away from key sessions; keep protein high that day. Set a cap (e.g., 1-2 drinks/week), choose lower-calorie options, and hydrate (1 glass water per drink). Avoid post-lift heavy drinking; allow at least several hours and include protein/carbs first. Protect sleep with an earlier cutoff.

:::checklist
- Limit | Keep drinks low, especially on training days.
- Buffer | Eat protein/carbs before drinking; hydrate between drinks.
- Sleep | Stop alcohol several hours before bed to reduce sleep disruption.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Binge drinking after training, blunting recovery.
- Ignoring added calories and loosening food choices.
- Relying on alcohol to unwind instead of improving sleep hygiene.
