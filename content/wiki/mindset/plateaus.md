## What is breaking plateaus?
Breaking plateaus means identifying why progress has stalled and adjusting training, nutrition, or recovery to restart forward momentum. Stalls are common and often signal the need for planned change, not failure.

:::takeaway
Check **sleep/stress**, tighten **nutrition** (protein/calories), adjust **volume/intensity** (add or deload), and refine **technique** before making big program overhauls.
:::

## How does it work?
Plateaus arise when the stimulus no longer exceeds adaptation or when recovery lags. Small changes—more load, reps, or sets, or a deload to reduce fatigue—restore [[progressive-overload]]. Improving execution or exercise selection can increase stimulus without massive volume jumps. Nutrition (adequate [[protein]] and calories) and [[sleep]] underpin recovery; deficits here can mimic training plateaus.
Stress, travel, or poor adherence can also masquerade as training stalls. Separating true strength plateaus from life and recovery noise prevents unnecessary program overhauls.

## Practical application
Audit recovery: 7-9 hours sleep, protein 1.6-2.2 g/kg, consistent calories. Review technique on key lifts; tighten range of motion and bracing. Choose one lever: add a set to priority lifts, add 2.5-5 kg or 1-2 reps where RIR is high, or deload for a week if fatigued. Swap a stale variation for a close cousin (e.g., pause squat) to resensitize. Track 2-3 weeks; only change one major variable at a time.
Check bar speed or RPE logs to ensure effort is aligned with goals; sometimes lifts plateau because sets drift to higher RIR than intended. Rein in distractions (phone use, long rest drift) to regain quality.

:::checklist
- Recovery audit | Confirm **7-9 hours** sleep and **1.6-2.2 g/kg** protein; align calories to goal.
- Stimulus tweak | Add **1 set** or **2-5% load/1-2 reps** where RIR is high; otherwise deload a week.
- Technique swap | Use close variants to refresh stimulus without abandoning core patterns.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Changing too many variables at once, hiding what worked.
- Adding lots of volume when fatigue is the real limiter; a deload would solve it.
- Ignoring technique and setup, assuming only load drives progress.
