## What is fitness for women?
Programming for women follows the same principles—progressive resistance, adequate [[protein]], and recovery—while considering menstrual cycle effects, bone health, and comfort.

:::takeaway
Use **progressive-overload**, **1.6-2.2 g/kg protein**, and adjust load/reps modestly if symptoms fluctuate across the cycle.
:::

## How does it work?
Hormonal shifts can affect energy, sleep, and perceived effort. Some women perform better in mid-cycle and prefer deload/light days during high-symptom phases. Strength and hypertrophy responses remain similar when training is consistent and protein is adequate.

## Practical application
Train 3-5x/week with compounds and accessories. If cramps/fatigue hit, lower load/reps or swap to accessories briefly, then resume progression. Prioritize calcium/vitamin D and impact work (jumps, loaded carries) for bone health as appropriate. Track symptoms to spot patterns and plan deloads. Use supportive clothing/equipment for comfort.

:::checklist
- Consistency | 3-5 sessions with structured overload.
- Cycle-aware | Flex load/reps during high-symptom days; deload as needed.
- Nutrition | **1.6-2.2 g/kg** protein; support bone health (calcium/vitamin D).
:::

## Common mistakes
- Assuming women need entirely different training; fundamentals are the same.
- Ignoring symptom patterns and forcing max loads on rough days.
- Under-eating protein and calories, impairing recovery.
