## What are isolation exercises?
Isolation exercises target a single muscle or small group with minimal involvement from other joints, such as biceps curls, leg extensions, or lateral raises. They sharpen mind-muscle connection and bring up weak points alongside compound lifts.

:::takeaway
Use isolation work **2-4 sets** per muscle, **8-20 reps**, at **1-3 RIR** to drive targeted growth without excessive fatigue.
:::

## How does it work?
Isolation lifts focus tension on a specific muscle by limiting joint movement and stabilizer demands. This improves activation, especially when combined with controlled tempo and [[mind-muscle-connection]]. Because loads are lighter than compounds, they create less systemic fatigue while still contributing to weekly [[volume]] and [[hypertrophy]].

## Practical application
Place isolation work after compounds to finish the target muscle without draining performance on big lifts. Use 8-20 reps with steady tempo (2-3s eccentrics) and short-to-moderate rests (60-120s). Track load or reps weekly to ensure [[progressive-overload]]. Choose angles and grips that suit your structure to avoid joint irritation. Keep most sets 1-3 reps in reserve; push an occasional top set closer to gauge effort.

:::checklist
- Slotting | Run isolations after compounds to target lagging muscles.
- Dose | **2-4 sets**, **8-20 reps**, **1-3 RIR** with controlled tempo.
- Progression | Add small load or 1-2 reps weekly while keeping tension on target.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Chasing feel with very light loads that never reach challenging effort.
- Letting momentum steal tension, reducing stimulus on the target muscle.
- Skipping isolation for weak points, leaving imbalances that limit compounds.
