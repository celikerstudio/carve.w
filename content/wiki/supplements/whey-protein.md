## What is whey protein?
Whey protein is a fast-digesting dairy protein rich in essential amino acids, especially leucine. It is convenient for hitting daily protein targets and supporting muscle repair. Compared with many whole-food proteins, whey offers high bioavailability and a strong anabolic signal.

:::takeaway
Use **20-40 g** servings to hit **1.6-2.2 g/kg** daily protein; pair **20-60 g** carbs post-workout for recovery.
:::

## How does it work?
Whey rapidly elevates blood amino acids and leucine, triggering muscle protein synthesis. Taken after training, it complements resistance work and [[progressive-overload]] to promote [[hypertrophy]]. Its convenience helps lifters meet total daily [[protein]] goals, which matter more than timing alone. Mixing whey with carbs can also speed glycogen recovery after high [[volume]] sessions.

## Practical application
Use whey to cover gaps when whole-food meals are inconvenient. Post-workout, take 20-30 g whey plus 20-60 g carbs to support repair and glycogen replenishment. Choose concentrate for cost, isolate for lower lactose, or hydrolysate only if you need faster digestion. Pair shakes with whole-food meals later to improve satiety and micronutrient intake. Pre-portion single servings in shakers to remove friction and protect [[consistency]].
For evening recovery, pairing whey with a slower protein like casein can extend amino acid availability overnight.

:::checklist
- Daily target | Hit **1.6-2.2 g/kg** protein; deploy **20-40 g** whey servings to fill gaps.
- Post-workout | Combine **20-30 g** whey with **20-60 g** carbs after training.
- Product choice | Use concentrate for value, isolate for low lactose, hydrolysate only if necessary.
:::

## Common mistakes
- Relying on shakes instead of building meals with fiber and healthy [[fats]].
- Under-dosing; 10-15 g often misses the leucine threshold for maximal synthesis.
- Ignoring total daily protein and assuming timing alone drives gains.
